# TermiSoc archive archaeology findings — 2026-09-18

## Purpose

Record useful leads found while reconstructing the early history of TermiSoc, particularly the period before and around 2006. These are research findings, not yet the finished public history.

## Findings

### TermiSoc existed by 2001

A January 2001 mailing-list archive contains a reference to a termisoc.org/~betty account. This is evidence that the termisoc.org domain and member web-space infrastructure existed by 2001.

**Source:** Mail Archive, January 2001, search result referencing termisoc.org/~betty.
https://www.mail-archive.com/search?f=1&l=london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk&o=newest&q=date%3A20010102

**Assessment:** Contemporary evidence for TermiSoc web infrastructure in 2001. The identity of 'betty' and the exact nature of the account require further investigation.

### Benjamin A'Lee — TermiSoc Tech Officer, February 2006

A February 2006 FreeBSD mailing-list message is signed by Benjamin A'Lee as **'Termisoc Tech Officer'** and includes the TermiSoc website.

**Source:** FreeBSD Questions mailing-list archive, 18 February 2006.
https://lists.freebsd.org/pipermail/freebsd-questions/2006-February/113401.html

**Assessment:** Contemporary evidence for the Tech Officer role and an operational TermiSoc web presence in 2006.

### Three Linux servers

A 2006 web reference associated with April Fools' Day on the Web lists a TermiSoc joke concerning moving its **three Linux servers** to Windows Server 2003.

**Source:** April Fools' Day on the Web, 2006.
https://aprilfoolsdayontheweb.com/2006.html

A later recollection by Keith Langmead describes himself as a principal TermiSoc sysadmin and recalls three Linux servers located in a university-building basement, with several students having root access.

**Source:** The Register Forums, user archive for Keith Langmead.
https://forums.theregister.com/user/1244/5/

**Assessment:** The server count and student-operated Linux infrastructure are supported by both a contemporary 2006 reference and a later recollection. The exact server names, hardware, location and operating history remain to be established.

### David Pithouse — member web/database services

A surviving PHP/MySQL tutorial by David Pithouse identifies him as **TermiSoc Secretary**. The material references phpmyadmin.termisoc.org and member web space under termisoc.org/~moose.

**Source:** SlideServe, 'MySQL and PHP Tutorial'.
https://www.slideserve.com/matteo/mysql-and-php-tutorial-powerpoint-ppt-presentation

**Assessment:** Strong evidence that TermiSoc provided member-facing web/database infrastructure and that David Pithouse held a committee role. The date of the tutorial and the identity behind ~moose should be confirmed from the original presentation or archive capture.

### Possible late-1990s origin

A later professional/education record for Dominic Norton associates him with the Plymouth computing society/TermiSoc during approximately 1997–2001.

**Source:** Dominic Norton public LinkedIn profile.
https://uk.linkedin.com/in/dominic-norton-786371131

**Assessment:** Retrospective evidence suggesting that the society predates 2001 and may have existed in the late 1990s. This should not yet be stated as an established founding date. Search for contemporary 1997–2001 evidence.

## Research implications

These findings shift the likely start of the reconstructed timeline earlier than 2006. The current evidence supports the following cautious chronology:

- **1997–2001:** possible/likely established computing society; further contemporary evidence required.
- **2001:** termisoc.org member web-space demonstrably existed.
- **2006:** TermiSoc had a Tech Officer, public web presence, and apparently three Linux servers.
- **2006 onward:** evidence points to substantial student-operated technical infrastructure.
- **Early 2010s:** later evidence shows a transition towards hacking, open source, web development, programming, electronics and maker activities.

## High-priority follow-up

1. Wayback Machine captures of termisoc.org, especially 1997–2010.
2. Historical DNS and subdomain evidence for termisoc.org.
3. Identification of the three Linux servers and their hostnames.
4. Identify 'betty' and 'moose' from surviving member pages or archive indexes.
5. Search 1997–2001 for contemporary TermiSoc references.
6. Reconstruct committee succession from Tech Officer, Secretary and other surviving role references.
7. Search old University of Plymouth/UPSU society listings and student publications.

## Evidence discipline

The above should remain in research/ until independently corroborated where possible. In particular, do not convert the late-1990s suggestion into a founding date, and do not treat the later recollection about the servers as contemporary evidence.
