# David Pithouse — research record

> **DRAFT — research record.** This file separates documentary evidence from the older legacy profile and from unresolved interpretation.

## Identity

David Pithouse studied Computing and Informatics at Plymouth University from 2000 to 2004, according to his current public LinkedIn profile. [^1]

The legacy TermiWiki/DokuWiki profile identifies him as **Dave “Moose/damoose” Pithouse** and records TermiSoc offices of PR Officer (2001–2002) and “Everything but the President” (2003–2004). Those office dates remain to be corroborated against contemporary society records.

## Direct TermiSoc evidence

A surviving PHP/MySQL tutorial presented by David Pithouse identifies him on its opening slide as **“Termisoc Secretary”**. The same tutorial references:

- `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org` as the TermiSoc-hosted phpMyAdmin service;
- `termisoc.org/~moose/tutorials` as Pithouse's TermiSoc-hosted tutorial area;
- `termisoc.org/~moose/tutorials/scary.txt` as an example file;
- `www.fluxbbs.org` as a source of help; and
- `termisoc@termisoc.org` as a TermiSoc contact address. [^2]

This is strong contemporary/surviving documentary evidence linking Pithouse, the **Moose** account, TermiSoc's web infrastructure, the general TermiSoc address, and the wider technical ecosystem.

The evidence does **not** by itself establish that Pithouse personally administered every service named in the tutorial. In particular, the appearance of `www.fluxbbs.org` establishes that it was recommended within this technical context, not that TermiSoc operated it.

## Infrastructure significance

The tutorial is useful beyond Pithouse's biography because it exposes several historical TermiSoc hostnames and services:

```text
termisoc.org
├── ~moose/tutorials
├── phpmyadmin.termisoc.org
└── termisoc@termisoc.org
```

`~moose` should be treated as a high-value target for further archival research. A surviving copy of the tutorial or an archived directory listing could potentially provide additional dates, project material, or links to other TermiSoc infrastructure.

## Relationship to the mailing-list research

The documented `termisoc@termisoc.org` address is distinct from the still-unresolved historical address `termilist@termisoc.org`. The tutorial confirms the former; it does not confirm the latter.

Separate 2001 evidence has established that TermiSoc-associated mailing-list infrastructure existed at `lists.termisoc.org` and `area51.termisoc.org`. That evidence should be recorded separately rather than inferred from Pithouse's tutorial.

## Research leads

1. Search the Internet Archive for `termisoc.org/~moose/` and descendants.
2. Search for copies or references to `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org`.
3. Search for `damoose`, `moose`, and `@termisoc.org` in surviving mailing-list archives.
4. Search specifically for contemporary evidence supporting the legacy 2001–2002 PR Officer and 2003–2004 office claims.
5. Investigate whether Pithouse's tutorial has a surviving original file, date, University module context, or publication metadata.
6. Keep `termilist@termisoc.org` as a separate unresolved mailing-list lead until a direct documentary reference is found.
