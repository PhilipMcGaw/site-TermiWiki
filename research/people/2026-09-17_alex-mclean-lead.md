User-supplied Facebook profile lead for **Alex McLean**, followed by web archaeology on the University of Plymouth student and later software-arts figure of that name. Facebook profile URL: https://www.facebook.com/share/1DgsHefbDK/?mibextid=wwXIfr

## Direct evidence

- A scholarly history of live coding states that **Alex McLean studied BSc Computing at the University of Plymouth**, where he met Adrian Ward, who was studying Media Lab Arts. It places both students at Plymouth and describes McLean as frustrated by the lack of creative opportunity in his applied computing course. Source: https://livecodingbook.toplap.org/book/
- The same history records McLean and Ward's collaboration under the name **slub**, including their September 2000 performance at the Institute of Contemporary Arts and the development of the Generative Manifesto. Source: https://livecodingbook.toplap.org/book/
- A 2000 paper, *The Aesthetics of Generative Code*, lists **Alex McLean, BSc**, with State51, London, alongside Geoff Cox of the University of Plymouth School of Computing and Adrian Ward. Source: https://slab.org/writing/The_Aesthetics_of_Generative_Code.pdf
- McLean's own 2009 retrospective says that in 2000 he formed slub with Adrian Ward and worked as a Perl hacker; it also says he left guideguide for State51 in 2001. Source: https://slab.org/2009/12/31/2000-to-2009/
- McLean's archived site identifies him as the author of later live-coding work, including Tidal and forkbomb.pl. These later technical activities are identity-continuity evidence, not evidence of TermiSoc membership.

## TermiSoc assessment

No independent web source found in this pass explicitly states that **Alex McLean was a TermiSoc member, officer, or organiser**.

The Facebook URL supplied by the user is therefore retained only as an identity lead. It is not treated as evidence of historical TermiSoc membership.

The Plymouth Computing connection makes McLean historically relevant to the wider computing environment around TermiSoc, but it must not be upgraded into a TermiSoc relationship without further evidence.
