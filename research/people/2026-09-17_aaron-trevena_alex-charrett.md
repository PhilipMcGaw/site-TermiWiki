# Aaron Trevena and Alex Charrett

## Source

Targeted public-web archaeology was conducted on 2026-09-17 following a lead connecting Aaron Trevena and Alex Charrett with TermiSoc and the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group. Searches used exact names, `TermiSoc`, Plymouth, the University of Plymouth, Linux User Group terminology, and historical mailing-list and Linux-community archives.

This record distinguishes contemporaneous documentary evidence from later retrospective profiles. No project-owner firsthand identification has been recorded for either person in the current TermiSoc member-identification source.

## Aaron Trevena

### Direct evidence

Aaron Trevena's current LinkedIn profile states that he attended the University of Plymouth from 1995–2000. Under activities and societies it identifies:

- `Termisoc - UPSU Computing and Internet Society : Treasurer and organiser`
- `Student Representative for BSc Hons Computer Systems and Networks`
- `Devon and Cornwall Linux User Group : Co-Founded the LUG with termisoc colleague Alex Charrett`
- `16 month Industrial Placement with MSAS Global Logistics - implemented web integration, intranet development, document management system.`

Source: Aaron Trevena LinkedIn profile.

URL: https://www.linkedin.com/in/aarontrevena

Aaron's personal website provides an independent retrospective account. Its open-source section says that the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group was co-founded with Alex Charrett in 1999, as part of running the University of Plymouth Computing & Internet Society. The education section also records his BSc (Hons) Computer Systems & Networks at the University of Plymouth, 1995–2000, and identifies him as Treasurer of the computing society.

Source: Aaron Trevena personal website.

URL: https://www.aarontrevena.co.uk/

There is also contemporaneous mailing-list evidence from 2001. In posts to the London Perl Mongers archive, Aaron Trevena's messages use a signature containing `http://termisoc.org/~betty` and `Betty @ termisoc.org`. This demonstrates that Aaron had a personal account/space under the TermiSoc domain at that time and provides a direct historical link between the individual and TermiSoc's web infrastructure.

Source: London Perl Mongers archive, messages by Aaron Trevena, including 10 April 2001 and 19 February 2001.

URL: https://www.mail-archive.com/london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk/msg02279.html

A broader search result for Aaron's 2001 London Perl Mongers messages preserves multiple examples of the same TermiSoc-domain signature, including messages dated January–May 2001.

Source: Mail-Archive search for messages from Aaron Trevena.

URL: https://www.mail-archive.com/search?f=1&l=london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk&o=newest&q=from%3A%22Aaron+Trevena%22

### Interpretation

The evidence establishes Aaron Trevena as a TermiSoc participant and officer during the late 1990s/early 2000 period, with his own current profiles identifying him as Treasurer and organiser. His historical TermiSoc-hosted personal web space provides particularly useful contemporaneous infrastructure evidence.

The personal website's statement that the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group was co-founded in 1999 is retrospective, but it is corroborated by a contemporary 2000 Linux Format directory listing the Devon & Cornwall LUG at `www.lug.termisoc.org`, with Alex Charrett as contact. The combination strongly supports a close organisational relationship between TermiSoc and the LUG at this time.

Confidence: **High** for Aaron Trevena's TermiSoc association and Treasurer/organiser role. **High** for his historical use of TermiSoc web infrastructure. **Medium–High** for the precise 1999 LUG founding date because the date is supplied retrospectively by Aaron rather than by a contemporaneous founding document.

### Interpretation

Alex Charrett is strongly evidenced as a TermiSoc participant in the 1996–2000 period. More importantly, the May and December 2000 Linux Format listings provide contemporaneous evidence that he was the named contact for the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group operating under the `lug.termisoc.org` domain. This is a useful primary-ish period source for reconstructing TermiSoc's relationship with the regional Linux community.

The 1999 founding date for the LUG should remain attributed to Aaron Trevena's retrospective account rather than presented as an independently established date until a contemporaneous founding record is found.

Confidence: **High** for Alex Charrett's TermiSoc association and his role as the 2000 Devon & Cornwall LUG contact. **Medium–High** for his participation in the 1999 LUG founding, based on Aaron Trevena's retrospective account and the subsequent contemporaneous LUG listings.

## Joint significance

Aaron Trevena and Alex Charrett appear to be an important early TermiSoc pair. The available evidence places them at the University of Plymouth during 1995/1996–2000 and connects them with the establishment and operation of the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group.

The `lug.termisoc.org` hostname is especially significant: it indicates that TermiSoc's own domain infrastructure was used to host or identify the regional Linux User Group. This should be treated as evidence of an organisational/infrastructural relationship, not automatically as proof that TermiSoc formally owned or controlled the LUG unless further governance evidence is found.

Aaron's 2001 TermiSoc-domain account (`termisoc.org/~betty`) also suggests that TermiSoc provided individual web space to members or officers. This may be a useful route into further people archaeology.
