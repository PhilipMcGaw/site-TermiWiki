# Marc Flamank / Mabs — Sun Lab and School of Computing

## Source

Primary corpus source: `source/Glossary/sun_lab.md`, DokuWiki source path `sites:termiwiki:sun_lab`, created in the migrated TermiWiki corpus on 2023-01-02. Retrieved 2026-09-17.

The page is a later DokuWiki-derived record of the 1990s environment, so its descriptions of 1997 are **later recollection/documentation**, not contemporaneous evidence.

External corroboration found 2026-09-17: a University of Plymouth repository thesis, *Performance of a Boussinesq Model for Shoaling and Breaking*, thanks **Mark Flamank**, “at the School of Computing, University of Plymouth”, for providing access to an FTP site and UNIX workstations. The source uses **Mark**, not Marc, so this is corroborating evidence for a Flamank in the School of Computing, but it must not yet be silently equated with Marc Flamank without further identity evidence. citeturn2search26

A 192.com directory result lists **Marc A Flamank** in Plymouth (electoral-register entries 2002 and 2014). This is a weak modern identity/location lead only. citeturn2search0

## Direct evidence from the TermiWiki Sun Lab page

The page states:

- Alex, Keith and Ross were in the Sun Lab in 1997.
- The “Sun Lounge” was a nickname used by some SOC technicians because some TermiSoc members spent substantial amounts of time there.
- The Sun Lab was described as the central location for TermiSoc until it was removed by the University in 1998 to make way for an NT4/Red Hat 6 lab.
- The lab was a **School of Computing laboratory**, containing Sun workstations including SPARCstation SLC, ELC and SPARCstation 1 systems.
- Crucially, it says: **“The Sun Lab was run by School Of Compting tech Flamank|Mabs”**, followed by a reference to his Unix beard.

The page therefore supports a distinction between University/School of Computing infrastructure and TermiSoc use of the space. It does **not** establish that the Sun machines belonged to TermiSoc, nor that TermiSoc administered the laboratory.

## Interpretation

The strongest present interpretation is that **Flamank, known in the TermiWiki corpus as “Mabs”, was a School of Computing technician who ran the Sun Lab**, while TermiSoc members used the University laboratory as a regular social/technical hangout.

The Sun Lab should therefore be treated as **environmental/institutional evidence**, not TermiSoc-owned infrastructure.

The external thesis evidence is particularly useful because it independently places a Flamank at the School of Computing providing access to FTP and UNIX workstations. However, because that source spells the first name **Mark**, identity with Marc Flamank remains unresolved.

## Publication impact

The published history should describe the Sun Lab as a **University/School of Computing space used heavily by TermiSoc members**, not as a TermiSoc machine room or server facility. Any future statement connecting Marc Flamank with the University should distinguish the strong corpus evidence from the unresolved Mark/Marc identity issue.
