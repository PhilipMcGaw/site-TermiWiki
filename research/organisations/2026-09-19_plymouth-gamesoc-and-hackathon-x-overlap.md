# PlymouthGameSoc and Hackathon Plymouth: X/Twitter overlap

## Scope

The official TermiSoc account followed both [PlymouthGameSoc (@PlymUniGameSoc)](https://x.com/PlymUniGameSoc) and [Hackathon Plymouth (@hackathonPlym)](https://x.com/hackathonPlym) when inspected on 19 September 2026. This record distinguishes direct evidence of overlap from plausible but unproven continuity or collaboration.

Following is evidence of a public account relationship at the time of inspection, not evidence of when it began, joint committee membership, or event co-hosting.

## PlymouthGameSoc

### Account evidence

The account displayed the name **PlymouthGameSoc**, was located in Plymouth, England, reported joining X in October 2015, and had 21 posts. Its initial rendered timeline consisted of October 2015 and January 2016 notices for Open Gaming, role-play, board games, and card games in **Babbage 003**.[^gamesoc-profile]

### Evidence of a TermiSoc connection

The strongest direct evidence is not the X follow: a TermiSoc flyer supplied by Matt Farrell describes **LAN parties jointly with GameSoc** in 2014.[^termisoc-flyer] The flyer establishes a planned or advertised joint activity, but does not by itself prove that `@PlymUniGameSoc` is the same organisation, as the X account reports joining in October 2015.

The two records nevertheless show a useful temporal and physical overlap:

| Date | Account | Observed content | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 October 2015 | TermiSoc | Thanks followers after a BCS Student Chapter talk, and promotes TermiSoc activity in the same week. | Direct TermiSoc activity evidence. |
| 12 October 2015 | TermiSoc | BCS Student Chapter talk in BGB105. | Direct TermiSoc Babbage-building activity evidence. |
| 12–25 October 2015 | PlymouthGameSoc | Repeated Open Gaming notices in Babbage 003. | Direct PlymouthGameSoc activity evidence. |

Both accounts therefore evidence society activity in the Babbage building during October 2015. This is **not** proof of a shared event, shared room, membership, or organisational continuity.[^termisoc-bcs][^gamesoc-october]

### Next evidence to seek

- A contemporaneous UPSU society listing or event page naming both GameSoc and TermiSoc.
- A post, flyer, AGM record, or personal recollection directly linking `@PlymUniGameSoc` to the GameSoc named in the 2014 TermiSoc flyer.
- Committee names or event photographs with independently verifiable people.

## Hackathon Plymouth

### Account evidence

Hackathon Plymouth described itself as bringing software developers, graphics designers, interface designers, project managers, and computer activists together. It listed Plymouth, linked `hackathonplymouth.org`, reported joining X in December 2012, and identified `@andy0cretan0` as its manager.[^hack-profile]

Its rendered timeline directly records:

- an October 2014 HackPlymouth event in Robbins, including a “tweet stream”;[^hack-october]
- a January 2015 event announcement;[^hack-january]
- a February 2015 hackathon with prizes provided by the School of Computing and Mathematics.[^hack-february]

### LinkedIn discovery

An indexed public LinkedIn profile identifies **Edmund Gentle** as a Plymouth University Computer Science student (2011–2014) who says he started a society called “Hackathon Plymouth” with course friends.[^edmund-linkedin] This is useful independent evidence that Hackathon Plymouth was a student-led initiative during the account’s active period, and identifies Edmund as a potential source for organiser and continuity information.

The profile does **not** name `@andy0cretan0`, mention TermiSoc, or establish a formal relationship between Hackathon Plymouth and TermiSoc. It should therefore be treated as an outreach lead and organisational-context evidence, not identity evidence for Andy Cretan.

### Relationship assessment

TermiSoc followed Hackathon Plymouth, and both accounts occupied the same local technical-society environment. The TermiSoc account also publicly thanked `@Andy0Cretan0` after the October 2015 BCS Student Chapter talk.[^termisoc-andy] Hackathon Plymouth’s profile identifies the same handle as its manager.[^hack-profile]

This supports a **documented public connection through Andy Cretan’s account**, but it does not establish that Hackathon Plymouth and TermiSoc jointly organised an event, that he held a TermiSoc committee role, or that the account manager was the speaker without a direct statement linking the two roles.

### Next evidence to seek

- The missing 2014 HackPlymouth event page and its participant or organiser credits.
- A reply from Edmund Gentle confirming the original organisers, whether `@andy0cretan0` was one of them, and any relationship to TermiSoc.
- TermiSoc posts referring directly to Hackathon Plymouth or `#HackPlymouth`.
- School of Computing and Mathematics, UPSU, or Internet Archive material naming organisers and venue.
