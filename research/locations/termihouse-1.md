# TermiHouse 1

## Research status

**Unresolved — dedicated search performed 2026-09-17.**

A dedicated web search was performed for `TermiHouse 1`, `TermiHouse`, `Termi House`, Plymouth, University of Plymouth, and TermiSoc combinations. The search did **not** locate a reliable public source that establishes what the historical TermiHouse 1 was, where it was located, who occupied it, or the dates of its use.

The exact term is heavily contaminated by unrelated modern uses of `Termihouse`, particularly a Mexican polyurethane/roofing business, so generic search results should not be treated as evidence for the TermiSoc subject.

## Direct evidence found

No directly relevant documentary evidence was located in the web search.

The search did, however, recover independent evidence that TermiSoc had a substantial student computing community at Plymouth. A 2011 *Register* forum contribution by Keith Langmead describes himself as having been the main sysadmin for the SU computing society, identifies it as TermiSoc, and says the society had three Linux servers stored in a university building basement. This is relevant background to the TermiSoc environment but does **not** establish a TermiHouse connection. Source: https://forums.theregister.com/forum/all/2011/06/14/sysadmin_it_disasters/ retrieved 2026-09-17.

A modern LinkedIn search result for Elly Kensington independently records TermiSoc membership at the University of Plymouth and a TermiSoc treasurer role, demonstrating that contemporary personal profiles can preserve useful TermiSoc history. This does not establish TermiHouse 1. Source: https://uk.linkedin.com/in/ellykensington retrieved 2026-09-17.

## Interpretation

`TermiHouse 1` should be treated as a **known research subject but presently undocumented in the public corpus**. It should not be promoted to a published historical claim until a source establishes its identity and relationship to TermiSoc.

The absence of search results is not evidence that TermiHouse 1 did not exist. Likely evidence may survive in material that is poorly indexed or no longer publicly searchable, including archived TermiSoc pages, personal websites, photographs, student accommodation references, mailing lists, social-media archives, or material held by former members.

## Research leads

1. Search the Internet Archive for `termihouse`, `termihouse1`, `termi house`, and likely TermiSoc web paths.
2. Search historical TermiSoc mailing-list and email-domain footprints for `house`, `termihouse`, and known member names.
3. Search personal websites and image archives belonging to former TermiSoc members, especially those already identified through firsthand recollection.
4. Search for combinations of `TermiHouse 1` with known TermiSoc member names rather than the term alone.
5. Investigate whether `TermiHouse` was an informal name for a particular student house and whether numbered houses existed.
6. Ask former members who lived there for the address, dates, occupants, photographs, and origin of the name; record such recollections explicitly as **firsthand testimony**, separate from documentary evidence.
7. Check archived University of Plymouth student/accommodation material for addresses or house names that can be correlated with the TermiSoc period.
