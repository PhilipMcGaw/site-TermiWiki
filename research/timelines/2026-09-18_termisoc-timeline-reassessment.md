# TermiSoc historical timeline reassessment

## Revised assessment

The previous working timeline was too conservative at the early end. The accumulated TermiWiki corpus supports treating **1991 as the working founding date**, with an explicit qualification that a later retrospective gives 1992. TermiSoc is therefore not best described as beginning in the mid-1990s or first becoming evidenced in 2001.

The strongest interpretation is of a long-lived student computing society whose character and infrastructure changed substantially over time.

### 1991–1995 — formation and establishment

The Abridged History, preserved from the TermiWiki/DokuWiki material and written retrospectively by former members, places the formation of the Terminal Society in 1991. It describes a BBS, Freshers recruitment, Union membership thresholds, an early society machine, and the development of the society's technical identity.

1994 provides particularly concrete evidence: Area51 was acquired as a society machine and the BBS was established under the Flux name. 1995 is deliberately obscure in the retrospective and should remain a low-evidence year rather than a presumed inactive year.

### 1996–2000 — mature infrastructure and formal continuity

Archived UPSU pages preserve the 1996–97, 1997–98 and 1998–99 executive committees. The 1997–98 committee included a Technical Officer and Projects Officer, with Ross Childs described as Head Sysop of Flux and Keith Langmead as the resident UNIX specialist.

The 1999 retrospective records purchase of the termisoc.org domain and continued Area51 infrastructure. In November 1999 the Devon & Cornwall Linux Users' Group was founded and hosted on Area51.

Contemporary January 2000 evidence identifies Alex Charrett as TermiSoc System Administrator and records TermiSoc hosting DCLUG infrastructure through lists.termisoc.org and area51.termisoc.org. The 2000 systems record documents Area51, Prot/Flux and Esra/DNS as a multi-server environment.

### 2000–2004 — decline/recovery cycle rather than disappearance

The Abridged History describes 2002 as a period in which "everything kinda died", followed by renewed organisation under Ryan Smith. 2003–04 is explicitly described as the "rebirth of Termisoc".

By 2004 there was a full AGM and a named executive including Rich Jeffery, Philip McGaw, Gemma Peter, Dan Cosser and Ryan Smith.

A May 2004 LinuxQuestions post by Rich Jeffery, identifying himself as TermiSoc President, provides contemporary evidence of active administration of the society's Linux servers.

The David Pithouse PHP/MySQL material further demonstrates member accounts, webspace and database services in this period.

### 2004–2006 — technically intensive infrastructure period

The 2005 server-failure record documents a major hardware failure of Apple, recovery to Arthur, subsequent failure/replacement of Banana with Trillian, and failure/rebuild of Orange as Ford.

The records show member web hosting, MySQL, mail, DNS, backups, a wiki, mailing-list infrastructure and an on-campus server cupboard.

The 2006 notices show Zaphod operating web/mail/database services, Arthur as projects/backup infrastructure, Trillian as DNS, and Joplin being used for backup work.

The historical personal-web namespace evidence is unusually strong: surviving references identify TermiSoc-hosted paths for betty, moose, j, andyk, harl, aande and penfold. This demonstrates a multi-user hosting environment, not merely a public society website.

### 2006–2010 — continuity with sparse surviving narrative

The old TermiWiki history page contains a blank/rough-draft heading for 2006–08, but the surviving infrastructure notices and archived site material demonstrate continued operation.

The archived TermiSoc site had a wiki, hosted-user pages, services and a TermiBlog; the site also documents TermiChat/IRC and TermiBot.

Ben A'Lee is contemporaneously identified as Tech Officer in February 2006. TermiBot and TermiChat connect the society's technical culture to IRC, scripting and member-developed software.

The correct interpretation is therefore **documentary gap, not known inactivity**.

### 2007–2010 — society/community life continues

A private-group photograph dated 26 August 2007 explicitly records the first "family" meal in TermiHouse 2. This provides direct evidence that a TermiHouse community existed by that date.

The surviving TermiSoc site and technical records bridge the gap between the 2006 infrastructure notices and the better-preserved early-2010s event evidence.

### 2010–2013 — strong documented activity and technical education

Nick Charlton's public profile identifies him as Safety Officer (2010–11) and Tech Officer (2011–13).

Siddharth Vadgama's retrospective records chairing TermiSoc for approximately two and a half years between 2011 and 2013, with 50+ person LAN parties, fortnightly guest tutorials and substantial promotional activity.

2011 TermiLan and Halloween photographs and the 2012 Freshers' LAN provide direct event evidence. A March 2012 Arduino Forum post identifies TermiSoc as the Plymouth University Computing and Technology Society and records an attempt to recruit Arduino guest speakers.

The society therefore appears to have combined traditional computing/server culture with programming, open source, electronics, Arduino, gaming and social events.

### 2014–2016 — mature student technology society

The 2014 Robot Wars social, 2014 external guest talk, and other event records show continued activity.

The 2015 executive graphic directly records a seven-person committee: Chairman Jamie Woods, Treasurer Phil Akers, Secretary Eleanor/Elle Milner, Health and Safety Harriet Eldred, BCS Liaison Mike Jones, Technical Officer Callum Trounce and Events Secretary Mike/Ducky Burgess.

The TermiSoc GitHub organisation preserves society software including TermiShell, TermiBot, Hubot, Capture the Flag, a nominations site, SkyPi and Mailman customisation. These repositories should be treated as technical primary-source archaeology.

### 2017 — explicit transition

On 22 February 2017, TermiSoc chairman Matt Farrell announced that TermiSoc was merging into Geek Society.

This is the clearest end-point for TermiSoc as a separately named society in the current evidence. The wording supports "merged into Geek Society", not "renamed Geek Society" and not "continued unchanged".

### 2017–2018+ — successor society, not yet proven direct continuity

Geek Society is independently documented in 2017 and the 2018 University of Plymouth Fresher material. Existing student profiles establish membership in Geek Society after the merger.

The present evidence does not justify treating every later computing/Geek/CompSoc organisation as a continuous legal or organisational continuation of TermiSoc.

## Key correction

The timeline should **not** start at 1995 or treat 2001 as the first meaningful evidence of TermiSoc. The corpus now contains a coherent chain from a claimed 1991 founding, through independently preserved late-1990s UPSU committee pages, contemporary 2000 systems administration evidence, the 2003–04 rebirth, the user's 2003–06 firsthand involvement, substantial 2005–06 infrastructure records, and the better-preserved 2010s material.

The main gaps are now:
- corroborating the exact 1991/1992 founding date;
- reconstructing 2000–03 officer succession and the 2002 decline/recovery;
- filling 2006–10 with primary evidence;
- establishing the relationship between TermiSoc, Geek Society and later CompSoc after 2017.

## Publication recommendation

The public history page should use the 1991 date with a qualification such as: "The surviving TermiSoc retrospective dates its formation to 1991; a later retrospective gives 1992." It should not state either date as an independently verified constitutional founding date until an earlier contemporary source is found.

The 2006–10 section should explicitly describe the evidence gap rather than imply inactivity.

The 2017 merger should be treated as a major transition point. Later Geek Society and CompSoc material should be linked as successor/related history without asserting unproven institutional continuity.
