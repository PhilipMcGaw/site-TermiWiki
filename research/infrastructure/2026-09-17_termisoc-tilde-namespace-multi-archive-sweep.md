# TermiSoc historical `~username` namespace sweep

## Source

A targeted multi-source web-archaeology pass was conducted on 2026-09-17 to identify historical personal web spaces hosted beneath the TermiSoc domain, especially URLs of the form `termisoc.org/~username/` and `www.termisoc.org/~username/`.

The search used general web indexing, preserved mailing-list archives, surviving third-party references, and indexed copies of historical links. Direct Wayback CDX/API access could not be completed in this pass because the available web interface rejected the unindexed CDX URLs as unsafe, and the local execution environment has no external DNS/network access. Therefore this record must **not** be interpreted as an exhaustive Wayback/Common Crawl/Memento enumeration.

## Direct evidence

### `~betty` — Aaron Trevena

Multiple preserved 2001 London Perl Mongers messages by Aaron Trevena contain the signature/link:

`http://termisoc.org/~betty` — `Betty @ termisoc.org`

The messages are dated January–June 2001 and repeatedly use the same TermiSoc-hosted URL. This independently corroborates the earlier finding that Aaron Trevena used a TermiSoc-hosted account named `betty`.

Source: Mail Archive, London Perl Mongers, Aaron Trevena message archive.

URL: https://www.mail-archive.com/search?f=1&l=london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk&o=newest&q=from%3A%22Aaron+Trevena%22

Confidence: **High** for the existence of the `~betty` namespace and its use by Aaron Trevena. The reason for the nickname `betty` remains unresolved.

### `~moose` — David Pithouse

A preserved PHP/MySQL tutorial by David Pithouse explicitly references:

`http://www.termisoc.org/~moose/tutorials`

and several files beneath that path, including `scary.txt`. The same artefact identifies David Pithouse as `Termisoc Secretary` and gives historical TermiSoc infrastructure such as `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org` and `termisoc@termisoc.org`.

Source: SlideServe, preserved presentation transcript.

URL: https://www.slideserve.com/matteo/mysql-and-php-tutorial-powerpoint-ppt-presentation

Confidence: **High** for the existence of the `~moose` namespace and its association with David Pithouse.

### `~j` — unidentified user

A 2003 PC Review discussion contains links to:

- `http://www.termisoc.org/~j/files/example.xls`
- `http://www.termisoc.org/~j/files/SWdata.xls`

The discussion is about spreadsheet/database work and identifies the posting user as `jago25_98`, but the available evidence does not establish that `j` and `jago25_98` are the same person, nor does it identify the person's real name. The URLs nevertheless provide direct evidence of another TermiSoc `~username` namespace.

Source: PC Review, "Search and replace 9000 times?", 10 November 2003.

URL: https://www.pcreview.co.uk/threads/search-and-replace-9000-times.958365/

Confidence: **High** for the existence of the `~j` namespace; **Unverified** for the person's identity and TermiSoc membership beyond the hosting path.

### `~andyk` — Andy K / ChemFan

A preserved chemistry-information reference identifies the English-language ChemFan site as:

`http://www.termisoc.org/~andyk/chemfan/`

The same reference states that the associated mailing list used `chemfan-request@termisoc.org` and `termisoc.org/cgi-bin/mailman/listinfo/chemfan`.

Source: Wikibooks, Chemical Information Sources, preserved reference material.

URL: https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Chemical_Information_Sources/SIRCh/Chemistry_Blogs_and_Discussion_Groups/Listserves%2C_Discussion_Lists%2C_and_Newsgroups_for_Chemistry

Confidence: **High** for the historical `~andyk` namespace and its association with ChemFan; **Unverified** for the person's full identity.

### `~harl` — Harl

Erowid preserves material originally hosted at:

`http://www.termisoc.org/~harl/tranqs.html`

The surviving page explicitly states that it was archived from that TermiSoc URL. This is direct evidence that another personal web space existed beneath the TermiSoc namespace.

Source: Erowid, preserved page.

URL: https://erowid.org/chemicals/barbiturates/barbiturates_info2.shtml

Confidence: **High** for the historical URL; **Unverified** for the person's full identity and exact relationship to the society.

### `~aande` — historical personal/caving page

A surviving Imperial College Caving Club page contains an external link to:

`http://www.termisoc.org/~aande/index.html`

The reference is embedded in an old external-links context and therefore provides evidence that `~aande` was a publicly reachable TermiSoc personal page. No reliable identity was established in this pass.

Source: Imperial College Caving Club, historical external-links material.

URL: https://imperialcaving.com/

Confidence: **High** for the existence of the historical URL; **Unverified** for identity and TermiSoc membership.

### `~penfold` — Deep Thought / Hitchhiker's Guide page

A surviving ClassicTV external-links page references:

`http://www.termisoc.org/~penfold/hhguide.html`

and describes it as the Deep Thought home page associated with a Hitchhiker's Guide resource. This provides evidence of another historical personal namespace under TermiSoc.

Source: ClassicTV, Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy external links.

URL: https://classictv.info/

Confidence: **High** for the historical URL; **Unverified** for the person's identity and TermiSoc membership.

### `mooseblaster.termisoc.org` — related historical host

Wikimedia Commons preserves a historical attribution for `Guru meditation.gif` pointing to:

`http://mooseblaster.termisoc.org/404.htm`

This is not a `~username` path, but it is relevant to the same historical hosting namespace and may provide a route to identifying the person behind `moose` or related TermiSoc hosting.

Source: Wikimedia Commons file history/attribution.

URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guru_meditation.gif

Confidence: **Medium** for its relevance to the TermiSoc hosting environment; **Unverified** that `mooseblaster` and `~moose` are the same account/person.

## Interpretation

The evidence strongly supports a historical TermiSoc hosting model in which individual users could publish material beneath a personal `~username` path. This is no longer merely an inference from two known members: at least seven distinct historical namespace names have now been recovered from independent surviving references.

The namespace appears to have been used for substantially different material: programming tutorials (`~moose`), open-source/Linux-related activity (`~betty`), a chemistry mailing-list site (`~andyk`), personal or specialist pages (`~harl`, `~aande`, `~penfold`), and downloadable working files (`~j`). This suggests that the TermiSoc server functioned as a general-purpose member web-hosting environment rather than only as the society's public website.

The names themselves must not automatically be treated as people's legal or preferred names. In particular, `betty`, `moose`, `harl` and `penfold` may be handles or nicknames.

## Archaeological significance

This namespace is potentially one of the most productive routes for reconstructing TermiSoc membership and activity because historical personal pages may contain:

- photographs and event reports;
- committee and officer information;
- personal project pages;
- links to other members;
- society mailing-list addresses;
- university locations;
- software and programming projects;
- historical files that are otherwise absent from the main TermiSoc site.

The `~j` result is especially useful because it demonstrates that even apparently mundane third-party forum references can preserve direct historical URLs and downloadable filenames.

## Next search strategy

The highest-value next step is not simply to query the root URL repeatedly. It is to treat the recovered namespace names as **seeds** and search outward:

1. Query each namespace in Wayback and other archives.
2. Search exact namespace URLs without the `termisoc.org` host to find third-party references.
3. Search the distinctive usernames (`betty`, `moose`, `andyk`, `harl`, `aande`, `penfold`, `j`) alongside Plymouth, University of Plymouth and TermiSoc.
4. Search filenames and subdirectories exposed by third-party references.
5. Follow inbound links from surviving external sites.
6. Record every newly discovered `~username` as a separate infrastructure/person lead before attempting identity resolution.

## Publication impact

The TermiSoc infrastructure history should document the existence of a multi-user personal web-hosting environment beneath `termisoc.org`. Individual namespace identities should remain separate from the general infrastructure claim until independently resolved.
