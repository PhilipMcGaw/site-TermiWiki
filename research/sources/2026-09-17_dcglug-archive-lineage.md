# Source

Web archaeology conducted 2026-09-17 against the surviving Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group (DCGLUG) website, its historical MHonArc archive, Linux Format LUG directories, and contemporary mailing-list material.

## Direct evidence

- The current DCGLUG site identifies the group as a Devon, Cornwall and South West GNU/Linux/free-software group with an email discussion list, meetings, Matrix, and IRC. Source: https://www.dcglug.org.uk/
- The LUG Directory maintained by lug.org.uk records **Devon and Cornwall** as established in **1999/10** and as active. Source: https://lug.org.uk/lugs/
- Linux Format, December 2000, lists **Devon & Cornwall**, URL `www.lug.termisoc.org`, with **Alex Charrett** as contact. Source: https://linuxformat.com/includes/download.php?PDF=LXF10.complete.pdf
- A surviving DCGLUG MHonArc archive exists at `archive-Nov00-May01`. Its individual messages preserve original list headers and infrastructure details. The archive therefore provides surviving primary-source mailing-list material from November 2000–May 2001, although the search conducted here did not recover an Alex Charrett-authored message.
- A 19 April 2001 archived message, “LDE: Linux Day Newsflash”, was addressed to `lug-list at area51.termisoc.org`, used `owner-lug-list at area51.termisoc.org` as sender, advertised `www.lug.termisoc.org`, and instructed subscribers to contact `majordomo at lists.termisoc.org`. Source: https://www.dcglug.org.uk/archive-Nov00-May01/msg00392.html
- The same April 2001 message describes the Devon & Cornwall LUG as organising/supporting the Exeter University Linux Day event and gives the event date as 29 April 2001. It also refers to speakers, sponsors, press releases, install requests, and volunteer assistance.
- Linux Format, September 2002, lists Devon & Cornwall with the URL `www.dclug.org.uk` and **Simon Waters** as contact, showing that by September 2002 the public LUG address/contact had changed from the TermiSoc-hosted `lug.termisoc.org` arrangement. Source: https://linuxformat.com/includes/download.php?PDF=LXF33.complete.pdf
- Aaron Trevena's later personal account says he co-founded the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group with Alex Charrett in 1999 as part of running the University of Plymouth Computing & Internet Society. Source: https://www.aarontrevena.co.uk/

## Interpretation

The surviving evidence supports a chronology in which the Devon & Cornwall LUG was founded in 1999, was publicly associated with the TermiSoc infrastructure by December 2000, and was still using TermiSoc-hosted list/domain infrastructure in April 2001. By September 2002, Linux Format records the LUG under `dclug.org.uk` with Simon Waters as contact.

This is evidence of an organisational/infrastructure relationship, not evidence that the LUG was itself a TermiSoc project. Aaron's account describes the LUG as being co-founded while he and Alex were running the University of Plymouth Computing & Internet Society; the contemporary Linux Format and mailing-list evidence independently demonstrates the use of TermiSoc-hosted infrastructure.

The surviving `archive-Nov00-May01` archive is particularly valuable because it preserves original mailing-list headers, hostnames, addresses, and contemporaneous event correspondence. However, the currently indexed surviving archive does not by itself establish that earlier 1999 material survives there.

## Corroboration

- `lug.termisoc.org`, `area51.termisoc.org`, and `lists.termisoc.org` occur together in the April 2001 primary-source archive message.
- Linux Format's December 2000 directory independently associates Alex Charrett with `lug.termisoc.org`.
- The September 2002 Linux Format directory provides a useful later boundary: `dclug.org.uk` and Simon Waters had replaced the earlier TermiSoc-hosted public address/contact in that directory.

## Open questions

- Does an archive from 1999–October 2000 survive elsewhere, particularly under an older D&C LUG domain or mailing-list host?
- Can Wayback or other historical web archives recover `lug.termisoc.org` pages, indexes, or member/contact information?
- Are there archived messages authored by Alex Charrett or Aaron Trevena in the November 2000–May 2001 archive that are not indexed by the current web search engine?
- What caused the move from `lug.termisoc.org` / `lists.termisoc.org` to `dclug.org.uk` by September 2002?
- Can the original TermiSoc-hosted LUG website or mailing-list configuration be reconstructed from archived DNS, URLs, or message headers?

## Publication impact

Useful for the TermiSoc infrastructure and external-community chronology. It provides a concrete bridge between TermiSoc infrastructure and the regional Linux community without implying ownership of the LUG by TermiSoc.
