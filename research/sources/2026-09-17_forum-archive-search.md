# Forum and technical archive search — TermiSoc

## Scope

Search of surviving technical forums and archive indexes for TermiSoc references, known people, aliases, infrastructure and historical assets. The aim was to find evidence not already represented in the research corpus.

## Direct evidence

### Rich Jeffery — LinuxQuestions

A May 2004 LinuxQuestions thread is authored by `Rich@Termisoc`. The author says he is attempting to add a user to the TermiSoc University of Plymouth CS servers, discusses root/password administration and the previous administrator's Fedora installation, and signs the post `Rich Jeffery / Termisoc President`.

Source: https://www.linuxquestions.org/questions/linux-general-1/adduser-and-passwrd-in-fedora-1-92-is-producing-errors-182976/

This is contemporary evidence of both office-holder status and hands-on administration of TermiSoc servers.

### Keith Langmead — The Register

A 2011 The Register forum contribution by Keith Langmead states that, while at university, he was the main systems administrator for the students' union computing society, explicitly identifying it as TermiSoc. He recalled that TermiSoc had three Linux servers of its own in the basement of a university building and that several people had root access. He described a historical recovery incident involving console access to one of the machines.

Source: https://forums.theregister.com/user/1244/5

Langmead said the incident was at least twelve years old when he wrote in 2011 and acknowledged that some details were fuzzy. The approximate late-1990s date should therefore remain retrospective rather than exact.

### Aaron Trevena — Mail Archive

The London Perl Mongers Mail-Archive search preserves repeated 2001 messages from Aaron Trevena using `termisoc.org/~betty` in his signatures and links. The archive also documents Aaron's contemporary Perl, web-development and LUG-related activity.

Source: https://www.mail-archive.com/search?f=1&l=london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk&o=newest&q=from%3A%22Aaron+Trevena%22

These records demonstrate Aaron's use of TermiSoc infrastructure during his period of involvement. They do **not** establish that his personal Perl/web projects were TermiSoc projects.

### TermiSoc wiki assets — Sublime Forum

A January 2016 Sublime Text forum post preserves two exact historical TermiSoc wiki image URLs:

- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/d/d3/Scrn1.jpg`
- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/a/a5/Scrn2.jpg`

Source: https://forum.sublimetext.com/t/build-system-to-run-in-cmd-exe/6149

The images themselves were not recovered during this search. They remain media-recovery leads.

## Negative findings

No useful new TermiSoc-specific Reddit result was identified in the broad search. Searches of indexed material for TermiSoc and known people/aliases did not establish a new historical identity for `penfold` or a new person-to-namespace mapping.

A Gazette record for a Keith Lewis Langmead in the Plymouth area was found during the search, but it is **not** treated as the TermiSoc sysadmin. The available evidence is insufficient to establish identity continuity.

## Interpretation

The technical forum ecosystem remains productive for TermiSoc archaeology, particularly where searches use individual names or aliases rather than only the literal term `TermiSoc`.

The strongest surviving forum evidence is:

1. Rich Jeffery's contemporary 2004 TermiSoc server-administration post.
2. Keith Langmead's later first-person account of TermiSoc's three Linux servers.
3. Aaron Trevena's contemporary 2001 `~betty` Mail-Archive footprint.
4. The surviving exact TermiSoc wiki image URLs.

## Open questions

- Can the `Scrn1.jpg` and `Scrn2.jpg` binaries be recovered from Internet Archive, Common Crawl, Memento or another archive?

- Can other root administrators mentioned by Keith Langmead be identified?

## Publication impact

The direct-source records should support the existing infrastructure archaeology. The negative Reddit result does not need publication, but is retained here to prevent repeated work being mistaken for a new lead.
