# Public email and mailing-list footprints for `termisoc.org`

## Verified public footprints

- The ChemFan directory page gives the English-language site as `www.termisoc.org/~andyk/chemfan/` and provides `chemfan-request@termisoc.org` as the subscription address. This supports a historical mailing-list service hosted on TermiSoc infrastructure, although the directory is a secondary source and does not by itself establish who operated the list.[^chemfan]
- A Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group archive preserves messages addressed to `lug-list at area51.termisoc.org` and gives `majordomo at lists.termisoc.org` for list administration. The footer identifies the list as the Devon & Cornwall LUG mailing list.[^dclug]
- A Mail Archive index preserves Aaron Trevena's 2001 London Perl Mongers messages, whose signature links to `http://termisoc.org/~betty` and describes the address as “Betty @ termisoc.org”. This is evidence that a TermiSoc-hosted personal address or alias was used in an external technical community; it is not evidence that the London Perl Mongers list was a TermiSoc list.[^london-pm]

## Lower-confidence leads

- A directory search result associates `case@termisoc.org` with an Andrew Burridge listing. This should be treated only as a lead until the underlying historical page, mailbox context, or a contemporary message establishes the address and its relationship to the TermiSoc Case alias.[^case-lead]
- Search results also show references to TermiSoc-hosted personal homepages in external mailing-list archives. These are useful for finding public activity, but an address appearing in a signature does not automatically prove membership, office-holding, or authorship of a TermiSoc project.

## Search limits

The public search results do not expose a complete list of TermiSoc mailboxes. Private or access-controlled Mailman archives, deleted messages, and addresses that were only used in local configuration will not necessarily be indexed. Searches for a particular person should therefore use the exact address, username, aliases, and known hostnames together, while preserving the original archive context.

No claim is made here that `skippy@termisoc.org` existed; that address would require a direct archival hit or another independent source.
