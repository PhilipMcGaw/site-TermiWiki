# TermiSoc namespace, infrastructure, institutional and event archaeology

## Purpose

This record captures the next systematic archaeology pass after the initial person-by-person work. It concentrates on surviving references to the old `termisoc.org` web namespace, infrastructure, institutional naming, events and independent corroboration.

## Direct evidence

### `~` user-space namespace

The surviving indexed web contains multiple references to individual TermiSoc user spaces and files:

- `~betty` — Aaron Trevena's historical web identity. A 2001 London Perl Mongers archive repeatedly preserves `http://termisoc.org/~betty`, including signatures and links associated with Aaron Trevena. Source: Mail Archive, 2001. https://www.mail-archive.com/search?f=1&l=london-pm%40lists.dircon.co.uk&o=newest&q=from%3A%22Aaron+Trevena%22
- `~moose` — David Pithouse's tutorial material. A preserved PHP/MySQL presentation identifies David Pithouse as TermiSoc Secretary and preserves `http://www.termisoc.org/~moose/tutorials`, `scary.txt`, `mysql1.php`, and related paths, as well as `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org`. Source: SlideServe, undated historical artefact. https://www.slideserve.com/matteo/mysql-and-php-tutorial-powerpoint-ppt-presentation
- `~j` — a 2003 forum post preserves `http://www.termisoc.org/~j/files/example.xls` and `SWdata.xls`. Source: PC Review, 10 November 2003. https://www.pcreview.co.uk/threads/search-and-replace-9000-times.958365/
- `~andyk` — the ChemFan page and archive were hosted under `http://www.termisoc.org/~andyk/chemfan/`; the same source preserves the TermiSoc Mailman subscription endpoint. Source: Wikibooks chemistry resource. https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Chemical_Information_Sources/SIRCh/Chemistry_Blogs_and_Discussion_Groups/Listserves%2C_Discussion_Lists%2C_and_Newsgroups_for_Chemistry
- `~harl` — Erowid explicitly states that its preserved barbiturate/tranquiliser page was archived from `http://www.termisoc.org/~harl/tranqs.html`. Source: Erowid. https://www.erowid.org/chemicals/barbiturates/barbiturates_info2.shtml

These are direct evidence that TermiSoc operated a multi-user web-hosting namespace rather than merely a single society homepage.

### Wiki uploads

A 2016 Sublime Text forum post contains two image URLs from the TermiSoc wiki:

- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/d/d3/Scrn1.jpg`
- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/a/a5/Scrn2.jpg`

The post describes them as screenshots used to illustrate a Python/Notepad++ workflow. Source: Sublime Forum, 13 January 2016. https://forum.sublimetext.com/t/build-system-to-run-cmd-exe/6149

The actual images were not recovered in this pass. They are recorded as asset-recovery leads, not recovered media.

### Infrastructure

Historical references establish several distinct TermiSoc-related hostnames:

- `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org` — preserved in David Pithouse's PHP/MySQL tutorial.
- `termisoc.org/cgi-bin/mailman/listinfo/chemfan` — preserved by Wikibooks.
- `lug.termisoc.org` and `lists.termisoc.org` — preserved in Linux Format directories and related Linux User Group material.
- `area51.termisoc.org` — preserved as the mailing-list host for Devon & Cornwall LUG in an April 2001 Linux Day announcement. Source: Devon & Cornwall LUG archive, 19 April 2001. https://www.dcglug.org.uk/archive-Nov00-May01/msg00392.html
- `mooseblaster.termisoc.org` — previously identified as a historical host lead; direct source should be re-retrieved before publication.

A 2006 April Fools index independently records a TermiSoc joke concerning moving “3 Linux servers” to Windows Server 2003. This is a secondary index and should not be treated as proof that the change actually happened. Source: April Fools' Day On The Web, 2006. https://aprilfoolsdayontheweb.com/2006.html

Separately, Keith Langmead's 2011 recollection says that, while at university, he was the main sysadmin for TermiSoc and that the society had three Linux servers in a university-building basement. He explicitly notes that the details were at least 12 years old and somewhat fuzzy. This is later recollection, but it is consistent with the 2006 indexed April Fools reference. Source: The Register Forums, 20 June 2011. https://forums.theregister.com/user/1244/5

### Rich Jeffery — direct infrastructure and office-holder evidence

A LinuxQuestions thread dated May 2004 is authored by `Rich@Termisoc`. The author says he is trying to add a user to the TermiSoc University of Plymouth CS servers, discusses root/password administration and the previous administrator's Fedora installation, and signs the post `Rich Jeffery / Termisoc President`.

Source: LinuxQuestions, May 2004. https://www.linuxquestions.org/questions/linux-general-1/adduser-and-passwrd-in-fedora-1-92-is-producing-errors-182976/

A separately preserved presentation titled *The Future of Video Gaming* identifies `Rich Jeffery President of TermiSoc`. The current SlideServe publication date is 2019 and therefore does not establish the presentation's original date; it is corroborating evidence only. https://www.slideserve.com/quincy/the-future-of-video-gaming-powerpoint-ppt-presentation

### TermiSoc events

A Royal Holloway research portal records a scheduled invited talk, *Programs hacking programs*, by David Repel to the Computing Society (TermiSoc) at the University of Plymouth on Monday 6 October 2014. This is independent institutional evidence of a TermiSoc event. Source: Royal Holloway Research Portal. https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/activities/programs-hacking-programs/

Nick Charlton's contemporaneous 2011 personal-site material remains a particularly valuable event source and should be linked from the existing person/event research rather than duplicated.

### Institutional naming

Current University of Plymouth course pages describe the present Computing Society as `CompSoc`, but this is modern evidence and must not be back-projected onto historical TermiSoc without additional evidence. Source: University of Plymouth course pages, retrieved 2026-09-17. https://www.plymouth.ac.uk/courses/undergraduate/bsc-mathematics-with-computer-science

Historical member records independently use several formulations, including:

- `Termisoc - UPSU Computing and Internet Society` — Aaron Trevena, University of Plymouth 1995–2000.
- `TermiSoc (University of Plymouth Computing Society)` — Nick Charlton, 2009–2013.
- `Termisoc (Plymouth University Computing and Technology Society)` — Leigh Brooks, 2012–2016.
- `Termisoc (Computing and Technology Society)` — other 2010s alumni records.

These records establish that the society's public descriptive name changed over time, but do not yet establish an exact formal renaming chronology.

## Interpretation

1. The `termisoc.org/~username` namespace was a substantive multi-user hosting service. It hosted personal pages, tutorials, downloadable files and specialist resources, not merely committee biographies.
2. TermiSoc appears to have operated or controlled significant computing infrastructure, including web hosting, mailing lists, database administration and Linux servers. The exact server topology and dates remain unresolved.
3. `area51.termisoc.org`, `lug.termisoc.org`, `lists.termisoc.org` and `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org` indicate that TermiSoc infrastructure supported activities extending beyond the immediate society, including the Devon & Cornwall Linux User Group and ChemFan.
4. Rich Jeffery provides strong contemporaneous evidence connecting a named office-holder with direct administration of the society's servers in 2004.
5. The evidence suggests several historical naming phases for the society, but the exact transition dates remain unresolved.
6. The discovery of the TermiSoc wiki image URLs is an important media-recovery lead. The images should not be represented as recovered until the original files or archived copies are obtained and hashed.

## Corroboration

- The 2004 Rich Jeffery server-administration record is consistent with the later Keith Langmead recollection of three TermiSoc Linux servers.
- The 2006 April Fools index independently mentions three TermiSoc Linux servers, although its statement is explicitly associated with an April Fools joke and therefore requires caution.
- The multiple independent `~username` references demonstrate that the user-space namespace was externally visible and actively linked during the early 2000s.
- The Royal Holloway event record provides independent institutional corroboration for TermiSoc activity in 2014.
