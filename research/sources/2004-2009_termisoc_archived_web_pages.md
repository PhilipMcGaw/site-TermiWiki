# Archived TermiSoc web pages, 2004–2009

## What was investigated

A set of Internet Archive snapshots supplied for comparison with the TermiWiki history, systems, UPSU:Radio, and WikiHistory pages.

## Direct evidence

- The 13 January 2005 history index links three first-person historical accounts: Case's early history, Chas's more recent history, and Moose's account of TermiSoc's “demise and rise”.[^history-index]
- Case's 15 January 2005 account describes the “Group Of Three” forming the Terminal Society around 1991: James Hickman, Marcus Adams, and Paul Fletcher. It also discusses the early Cadmus machine, a Cheeseplants House MUD meet, and Hades as an early TermiSoc BBS. This is retrospective recollection published by “Case”, not contemporary 1991 evidence.[^case-history]
- Chas's 15 January 2005 account lists the 1996 executive, describes Flux receiving its own machine (Prot), and identifies Alex Charrett, Jeff Powell, and Ross Childs as members who joined in September 1996. This is also retrospective recollection.[^chas-history]
- Moose's 15 January 2005 account describes the 2000 and 2001 executive arrangements, a 2001 membership fee of £1 with about 40 members joining, the subsequent collapse in activity, and the 2002 involvement of Ian Christian and Theo Z. It also mentions Area51 and Esra being revived.[^moose-history]
- The 17 December 2004 kit list says TermiSoc ran Apple, Orange, and Banana, with Orange providing the CD library and secondary DNS/backup functions. It describes Kumquat as the founding machine for UPSU Radio and a planned test machine for an external TermiSoc project with UPSU and MLA.[^kit-list]
- The 19 February 2005 policy page lists a System User Agreement, Code of Conduct, Constitution, Safety Statement, Mission Statement, and Security Policy.[^policy]
- The 7 March 2005 front page describes TermiSoc as the University of Plymouth Computing Society, says it had existed since circa 1994, and lists shell accounts, web and email services, weekly socials, fortnightly tutorials, and links with Devon and Cornwall LUG and ex-members.[^front-page]
- The 15 February 2005 Integrem snapshot describes a Plymouth IT business offering business-management software, hardware, networking, and support. The relationship, if any, to TermiSoc is not established by this snapshot alone.[^integrem]
- The 15 January 2006 WikiHistory main page describes the project’s scope as preserving TermiSoc’s past, present, and future, and lists sections for members, executives, socials, tutorials, projects, systems, glossary, myths and legends, history, and related documentation.[^wikihistory-main]
- The 29 September 2007 TermiSoc page describes the society as UPSU’s Computing Media Society, its student membership, technical services, tutorials, three-server arrangement, DNS responsibilities, and academic backing. The supplied snapshot truncates before the full account of that backing.[^termisoc-wiki]

## Unresolved or limited retrievals

The 2009 snapshots for `Category:Tutorials`, `History`, and `Special:Wantedpages` returned archive compression errors during retrieval. Their URLs remain useful leads, but no claims should be based on their contents until they can be recovered or corroborated.

The supplied `wiki.termisoc.org` 2006–2009 pages are later WikiHistory material and should be distinguished from contemporary evidence about the events they describe.
