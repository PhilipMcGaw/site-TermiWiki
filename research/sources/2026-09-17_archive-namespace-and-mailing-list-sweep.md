## Direct evidence

### Aaron Trevena / `~betty`

The London Perl Mongers archive contains numerous Aaron Trevena messages from 2001 carrying the signature/link `http://termisoc.org/~betty`. The archive includes messages dated January through June 2001. One 10 April 2001 message states that he had uploaded AutoDIAL to his site; other messages discuss Perl, CGI, MySQL, web development and Linux/Open Source subjects. A 3 June 2001 message records his move to Bradford-on-Avon while retaining the TermiSoc-hosted identity in the signature.

This is contemporary evidence that a TermiSoc-hosted personal web namespace was actively used by Aaron Trevena in 2001. It does not establish that the software discussed in every message was a TermiSoc society project.

### TermiSoc mailing-list infrastructure

A 19 April 2001 Devon & Cornwall LUG archive message has `lug-list at area51.termisoc.org` as the mailing-list address and identifies `www.lug.termisoc.org` as the LUG website. The message also states that mail could be sent to Majordomo at `lists.termisoc.org`. This provides contemporary evidence for multiple TermiSoc-hosted subdomains/services in April 2001.

### Historical wiki image paths

A 13 January 2016 Sublime Text forum post preserves two exact historical TermiSoc wiki asset URLs:

- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/d/d3/Scrn1.jpg`
- `http://termisoc.org/wiki/uploads/a/a5/Scrn2.jpg`

The post says the author used PyNPP and links these images as screenshots. The post is late secondary evidence for the existence of those URLs, not evidence of the original upload date or photographer/author.

### 2006 April Fools reference

The 2006 April Fools Day On The Web index lists `termisoc.org` with the joke: `3 Linux servers over to Windows Server 2003.` Because the page is explicitly an April Fools joke index, this is not evidence that TermiSoc actually migrated three servers. It is nevertheless a useful historical pointer because it independently confirms that `termisoc.org` was an active recognisable site by 2006.

## Interpretation

The archive sweep strengthens the picture of TermiSoc as an Internet-facing student computing society with its own web hosting and several related services by at least 2001. The infrastructure included personal Unix-style web namespaces, a LUG site, mailing-list services, and later a wiki with uploaded media.

The exact chronology of the infrastructure remains unresolved. In particular, the search results do not yet provide a complete Wayback/CDX inventory of `termisoc.org`, nor do they recover the contents of the two historical wiki images.

The Aaron Trevena material is particularly useful because it gives repeated, independently archived contemporary references rather than a single retrospective biography.

## Corroboration

The April 2001 D&C LUG archive corroborates the TermiSoc-hosted LUG/list infrastructure independently of TermiSoc's own pages. Existing TermiSoc archaeology also contains later evidence for `phpmyadmin.termisoc.org`, `~moose`, `~j`, `~andyk`, and other namespaces.
