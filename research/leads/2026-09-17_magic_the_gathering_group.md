# Magic: The Gathering group at the University of Plymouth

## Investigation

Searched public web-indexed UPSU, University of Plymouth, LinkedIn, and Facebook results for Magic: The Gathering, MTG, and related society names. Retrieval date: 2026-09-17.

## Findings

- The current [UPSU Games Society page](https://www.upsu.com/societies/GamesSociety/) explicitly identifies Trading Card Games as one of the society's sections, including Magic: The Gathering. It describes regular Magic drafts, tournaments, themed challenges, and a Magic taster day. It also lists Commander (EDH), cEDH, and Pauper groups. **Confidence: High** for a current organised MTG group within Games Society.
- The same page lists a Magic: The Gathering Taster Day in Rolle 210, and gives the Games Society email, Discord, Instagram/linktree, committee, and membership information. **Confidence: High** for the current society's public contact and event evidence.
- The [UPSU Games Society page](https://www.upsu.com/societies/GamesSociety/) states that the Games Society has existed since before the University of Plymouth, but this is an undated institutional claim and should not be used as a precise foundation date. **Confidence: Low–Medium** for the historical longevity claim.
- The 2018 [UPSU fresher's booklet mirror](https://www.readkong.com/page/medsoc-2018-fresher-s-booklet-7259962) lists “Games Society” among University of Plymouth societies. This supports the existence of a Games Society at that date, but does not itself prove that MTG had a separate committee or regular programme then. **Confidence: Medium**.
- Searches did not find reliable evidence of a separate society named “Magic: The Gathering Society” or “MTG Society”. The abbreviation “MTG” in University of Plymouth material commonly refers to the Musical Theatre Group, so it should not be treated as evidence of a card-game group without context. **Confidence: Medium–High**.

## Interpretation

There is strong evidence for an organised Magic: The Gathering community at the University of Plymouth, but the available evidence places it inside the Games Society rather than as a separate standalone society. The current page suggests a substantial and structured card-gaming programme, while the 2018 Games Society listing provides earlier continuity for the host society. The date when MTG activities first became a documented Games Society section remains unresolved.

## TermiSoc connection search

- [Matt Farrell](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/matt-h-farrell) lists both “TermiSoc (Computing Society)” and “Games Society” among University of Plymouth activities for 2012–2019. The same profile records him as TermiSoc chairman and later treasurer from 2014–2017. This is evidence of at least one person participating in both societies, but it does not establish that TermiSoc controlled, founded, sponsored, or formally partnered with Games Society or its MTG activities. **Confidence: Medium**.
- [Mikey Legg](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/mlegg) similarly lists “Termisoc - Technology Society” and “Gaming Society” among University of Plymouth activities for 2014–2018. This independently supports possible membership overlap, but not an organisational connection. **Confidence: Medium**.
- Searches for a direct TermiSoc–MTG or TermiSoc–Games Society partnership, shared committee, event, or funding record did not produce reliable evidence. **Confidence: Medium** for this negative search result only.

## Revised interpretation

The available evidence supports a social and membership overlap between TermiSoc and Games Society, but not a formal organisational relationship. At least two alumni records name both groups, and one belongs to a TermiSoc committee officer. The MTG programme is documented as part of Games Society; no source found so far attributes it to TermiSoc.
