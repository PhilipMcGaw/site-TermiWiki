# Robotics Society

The **Robotics Society**, historically referred to as **RoboSoc**, is a University of Plymouth students' society concerned with robotics and practical robot projects.

## Historical relationship with TermiSoc

RoboSoc and TermiSoc were separate societies during at least the 2012–2016 period. University of Plymouth student records independently list students as members of both societies. For example, Milutin Manojlovic's university record lists RoboSoc (Robotics Society) and TermiSoc, while Elly Kensington records joining both societies and later serving as TermiSoc treasurer.[^1][^2]

This distinction is important: RoboSoc should not be treated as an alternative name for TermiSoc.

## Evidence of later RoboSoc activity

The evidence extends beyond the 2012–2016 student records. A 2020 Students' Union honours listing names a student under **Techsoc and Robosoc**.[^3] A 2021 University publication states that **RoboSoc was reviving** the humanoid robot Scott, which had stood for the first time in February 2020.[^4] A 2022 Students' Union societies listing uses the form **Robotics (RoboSoc)**.[^5]

This provides a documented sequence of the RoboSoc/Robotics Society name from the earlier student records into the 2020s. It does not, by itself, prove uninterrupted organisational continuity: the 2021 source's wording about RoboSoc "reviving" may indicate a period of inactivity or reactivation, and the available sources do not yet provide a constitutional or committee-level link across the gap.

## Humanoid robotics

University of Plymouth has a longer history of student humanoid-robot work. The 2021 publication records a history of University of Plymouth humanoid robots competing between 2004 and 2015, and describes RoboSoc's work on Scott.[^4]

## Current society

The current Students' Union page uses the name **Robotics Society**. It describes the society as open to students from all disciplines and backgrounds, with activities intended to support learning, experimentation, collaboration, and innovation. Its principal event is Combat Bots.[^6]

The University of Plymouth also currently promotes the Robotics Society as a student activity associated with its robotics provision.[^7]
