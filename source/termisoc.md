# TermiSoc

TermiSoc was the University of Plymouth Students' Union computing society, known at different times as the Terminal Society, Computing Society, Computing and Technology Society, and, during its later period, the Computing & Gaming Society (CGS). The surviving evidence points to a society centred on computing, Linux and open-source software, networking, web development, electronics, technical teaching, projects, and social events.

The historical society is no longer operating under the TermiSoc name. Its later history includes a 2016 transition to CGS and a 2017 merger into Geek Society. The present-day University of Plymouth Computer Society (CompSoc) is recorded separately; the available evidence does not establish that it is the same legal or organisational entity as historical TermiSoc.

## Chronology

The entries below are deliberately **reverse chronological**. Detailed committee records, technical systems, events, and source material should live on their dedicated pages rather than being repeated here.

### 2017 — merger into Geek Society

In February 2017, TermiSoc announced that it was no longer going forward and had merged into Geek Society. The announcement directed paid TermiSoc members to Geek Society's AGM and described a proposed Geek Society Technical Officer role covering LANs, equipment, and online servers such as Minecraft.[^1]

The available evidence supports recording Geek Society as the society into which TermiSoc merged, rather than treating it as simply another name for TermiSoc.

See [[geek_society]].

### 2016 — Computing & Gaming Society

By 26 October 2016, the official TermiSoc Facebook group described itself as the **Computing & Gaming Society (CGS), formerly TermiSoc**. It listed Jacob Gathercole as Chairman, Rachael Tordoff as Secretary, Matt Farrell as Treasurer, and Chris Brown as Safety Officer.[^2]

This establishes CGS as the operating name at that point. The formal process and exact date of the re-brand remain unconfirmed.

### 2014–2016 — later TermiSoc

A 2014 committee flyer lists Matt Farrell as Chairman and Jacob Gathercole as Treasurer and describes workshops, socials, hackathons, projects, LAN parties with GameSoc, and movie nights.[^3] A separate flyer announced a First 24 Hour LAN for October 2014; the announcement establishes the planned event, rather than confirming that it took place.[^4]

The 2015–2016 committee is independently recorded on the [[Glossary/termiexec|TermiExec]] page.

In 2014, the University required the society's server to be removed from its cupboard. A later attempt to revive the society did not recover all of the old equipment. The surviving recollection records another demise around 2015 and a further attempt to restart TermiSoc for 2016.[^5]

### 2011–2013 — tutorials, projects, and LANs

Siddharth Vadgama's later retrospective records him chairing TermiSoc for two and a half years between 2011 and 2013. He describes weekend projects, 50+ person LAN parties, fortnightly guest-led tutorials, and production of society promotional material.[^6]

A March 2012 Arduino Forum discussion also identifies TermiSoc as the Plymouth University Computing and Technology Society and records an attempt to obtain Arduino guest speakers.[^7]

### 2004 — active technical and social period

The 2004 AGM produced:

- Rich Jeffery — President
- Philip McGaw — Secretary & Safety Officer
- Gemma Peter — Head Technical Officer & Webmaster
- Dan Cosser — Treasurer
- Ryan Smith — Assistant Technical Officer

The preceding months are described as busy, with server upgrades, member drives, tutorials, socials, and trips to trade shows.[^8]

### 2003 — revival

In September 2003, an executive committee was recorded without an AGM:

- Ryan Smith — President
- David Pithouse — Secretary
- Jared Somerville — Treasurer
- Alex — Safety Officer

After further changes, the committee became Ryan Smith (President), David Pithouse (Secretary), Andrew Pearce (Treasurer), and Rich Jeffery (Safety Officer). A successful Freshers' Fayre and renewed activity are described in the surviving retrospective.[^8]

### 2002 — decline and reorganisation

The 2002 committee is recorded as Ian Christian (President) and Z (Secretary), with Ian Christian and Theo Zourzouvillys maintaining the servers after Area51 and Esra failed.

Later in 2002, a new committee formed around Ryan Smith (President), Ian Christian (Head Technical Officer), and Z (Secretary).[^8]

### 2001 — reduced committee

The 2001 AGM produced Adam Ward as President, David Pithouse as PR Officer, Sebastian Swainsbury as Treasurer, and Sam Burrage as Head Technical Officer. A later account records a reduced committee in which David Pithouse held Secretary, Treasurer, and Head Technical Officer responsibilities.[^8]

### 2000 — infrastructure and community

The 2000 AGM produced Alistair Cowan (President), Adam Ward (Secretary), Malcolm Kelly (Treasurer), Jon Still (Technical Officer), and Pete Hatton (PR & Training Officer).

Contemporary and retrospective evidence also connects TermiSoc with technical services and the wider local computing community. A January 2000 DCLUG mailing-list message identifies Alex Charrett as TermiSoc System Administrator and records TermiSoc hosting DCLUG list infrastructure through lists.termisoc.org and area51.termisoc.org.[^9]

### 1999 — termisoc.org and DCLUG

The 1999 AGM produced:

- Ross Childs — President
- Richard Pettigrew — Secretary
- David Ellams — Treasurer
- Alex Charrett — Technical Officer
- Marc Ashworth — Projects & Training Officer
- Scott Grenny — PR Officer

The society acquired the termisoc.org domain during this period. A surviving Network Solutions/InterNIC invoice records a two-year TERMISOC.ORG registration beginning in July 1999; it supports the 1999 registration evidence but does not independently establish that this was the domain's first registration.[^10]

TermiSoc also hosted the Devon & Cornwall Linux Users' Group (DCLUG) on Area51, providing a documented connection between the society's infrastructure and the wider local Linux community.[^8][^9]

### 1998 — established society infrastructure

The 1998 AGM elected Jon Still (President), Robert Callaghan (Secretary), Pete Hatton (Treasurer), Keith Langmead (Technical Officer), Tiley Marner (PR Officer), and Alex Charrett (Projects Officer).

During the year, Pete Hatton took over PR duties after Tiley Marner left the University.[^8]

### 1997 — rapid growth

The 1997 AGM elected Jeff Powell (President), Alex Charrett (Secretary), Aaron Trevena (Treasurer), Keith Langmead (Technical Officer), Gareth Alden (PR Officer), and Ross Childs (Projects Officer).

The society acquired another server, Esra, and the surviving account describes a particularly successful 1997 Freshers' Fayre, with approximately 60 members joining on the evening. TermiSoc also developed a closer relationship with Computing Science and used Flux for communication and feedback.[^8]

### 1996 — new servers and members

The 1996 executive included Jason Gould (President), Alex Stanley (Secretary), and Alex McLean (Technical Officer). Treasurer and PR responsibilities changed during the year.

TermiSoc acquired another server, Prot, for the BBS. Three new students — Alex Charrett, Jeff Powell, and Ross Childs — also joined during September and became significant figures in the society's subsequent history.[^8]

### 1995

The surviving Abridged History contains no substantive account for 1995 beyond the statement that it was a year the author did not discuss.[^8]

### 1994 — Area51

The 1994 Freshers' Fayre was a major recruitment push, with more than 30 members reportedly joining by the end of the evening. The Union subsequently provided funding for TermiSoc's first society-owned machine, Area51, a 486 DX50 with 8 MB of memory.

The BBS software was also renamed simply BBS.[^8]

### 1993 — factions and the first machine request

The 1993 executive was eventually recorded as Andrew Burridge (President and Technical Officer), Paul Bland (Secretary and Technical Officer), Alex McLean (Treasurer and Technical Officer), and Marcus Adams (Technical Officer).

The society submitted its first budget request for a machine and membership again exceeded 20 people.[^8]

### 1992 — early membership

Freshers' Fayre 1992 reportedly brought TermiSoc to 12 members. The executive changed during the year, with James Hickman, Robert Dymond, Marcus Adams, Jon Care, and Andrew Burridge appearing in the surviving account.

Membership subsequently reached the low twenties, helping the society avoid being removed under Students' Union rules.[^8]

### 1991 — claimed foundation

The surviving Abridged History places the formation of the original Terminal Society in **1991**, by James Hickman (President), Marcus Adams (Technical Officer), and Paul Fletcher (Treasurer).

This date is not uncontested. A later retrospective by Siddharth Vadgama gives **1992** as the establishment year. Both claims are retained in the research record pending an earlier primary source.[^11]

## What TermiSoc provided

The surviving material records a broad range of member services and activities over its lifetime, including:

- Linux shell accounts and server access;
- web hosting, including PHP and CGI;
- email accounts on @termisoc.org;
- MySQL and PostgreSQL services;
- technical support and discussion forums;
- tutorials and workshops;
- programming and open-source projects;
- LAN parties, gaming, socials, and trips;
- electronics and other technical projects.

These were not necessarily all available at the same time. The detailed service, project, and infrastructure records should be consulted for period-specific evidence.

See [[systems]], [[projects]], [[tutorials]], and [[termisoc_external_evidence]].

## Related societies and communities

### Computing Society / CompSoc

The present-day University of Plymouth Computer Society (CompSoc) is documented separately on the site. The available evidence establishes a related computing-society tradition, but does not establish uninterrupted organisational continuity with historical TermiSoc.

### Robotics Society / RoboSoc

RoboSoc was a separate society from TermiSoc. Contemporary University of Plymouth records from 2012–2016 independently list membership of both societies. Later evidence shows the RoboSoc/Robotics Society name continuing at Plymouth, but does not by itself establish uninterrupted organisational continuity.[^12]

### Devon & Cornwall Linux Users' Group

TermiSoc's infrastructure hosted DCLUG services around 2000, providing direct contemporary evidence of a connection between the two communities.[^9]
