This page records independently hosted material useful for reconstructing TermiSoc history. It distinguishes contemporary material from later recollections and preserves conflicting dates rather than selecting one without further evidence.

## Origin date: two recorded claims

- [[../history|History]] and the local Abridged History place the society's founding in **1991**.
- [[siddharth_vadgama|Siddharth Vadgama]]'s later project retrospective says it was **established in 1992**.

Both dates are retained pending an earlier primary record.

## 2000: technical services and DCLUG hosting

A DCLUG mailing-list message from [[alex_charrett|Alex Charrett]], dated 11 January 2000, identifies him as **TermiSoc System Administrator**. The message shows that TermiSoc hosted DCLUG list infrastructure through `lists.termisoc.org` and `area51.termisoc.org`, and that Alex had a personal `termisoc.org` web page. This is direct contemporary evidence of TermiSoc's early systems role and links with DCLUG.

- [DCLUG archive: “Free Linux ISP”, 11 January 2000](https://www.dcglug.org.uk/archive-old/msg00036.html)

## Member services, teaching and socials

An eight-slide TermiSoc presentation describes the society as covering computers, gaming, media, electronics, digital DJing and film. It lists **500 MB web space**, personal email accounts, a technical-support mailing list, tutorials/workshops, gaming events, digital-DJ nights, road trips and BBQs. It also advertises an all-day LAN/console party at United Sherwell Church on a Saturday 20 October; the transcript does not establish the year. The SlideServe upload date is not treated as the presentation's original date.

- [“Who are we?” presentation transcript](https://www.slideserve.com/hedwig/who-are-we-powerpoint-ppt-presentation)

## 2011–2013: chairmanship, tutorials and LAN events

[[siddharth_vadgama|Siddharth Vadgama]] records chairing TermiSoc for two and a half years between **2011 and 2013**. His retrospective describes events from weekend projects to **50+ person LAN parties**, fortnightly guest-led tutorials, and his production of society promotional material, including freshers', LAN-event and propaganda-style posters.

- [Siddharth Vadgama: TermiSoc project retrospective](https://www.siddv.net/projects/termisoc/)

## 2012: Arduino outreach

In a March 2012 Arduino Forum discussion, a participant identifies TermiSoc as the **Plymouth University Computing and Technology Society** and says it was seeking Arduino guest speakers through “Arduino no 13” on its announcements list. This supports the record of technical workshops and guest-led teaching.

- [Arduino Forum: South West UK, March 2012](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/south-west-uk/92569/5)

## Public software projects

The public [TermiSoc GitHub organisation](https://github.com/termisoc) identifies itself as “Plymouth University Computing Society”, based in Plymouth. It retains seven repositories, including:

- `termi` — an extensible Linux shell, also called TermiShell;
- `termibot` — the IRC bot for `#termisoc`;
- `hubot` — a TermiSoc Slack bot;
- `capturetheflag` — a real-world Capture the Flag web application;
- `Nominations-Site` — an archived nominations site attributed to [[nick_charlton|Nick Charlton]];
- `SkyPi` — a weather-balloon project; and
- `mailman-theme` — customisations to Mailman's public HTML.

The `termi` repository has **no GitHub releases**, so the releases page does not provide version history.

- [TermiSoc GitHub organisation](https://github.com/termisoc)
- [TermiShell repository](https://github.com/termisoc/termi)
- [TermiShell releases](https://github.com/termisoc/termi/releases)
